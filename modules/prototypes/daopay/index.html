Daopay user role - EU merchant onboarding

A scoped role that lets the Daopay compliance team see their own merchant applications and make the acquiring decision themselves. They read everything and can add documents; they make exactly one decision. Everything that generates a document, calls an integration or overrides a screening result stays with PXP.

In design Owner: Product (onboarding) Acquirer: Daopay (EU)

Why this exists

Daopay is the acquirer for EU merchants, and their compliance function is separate from PXP underwriting. This role puts the decision where the accountability already sits: the Daopay compliance team logs in, opens the application they are being asked to accept, reads the same evidence PXP read, and decides. Nothing about the application changes - same portal, same page, same evidence. The only difference is who is looking at it and which buttons they get.

How it works, end to end

Steps 1 to 7 are PXP and the platform. Step 8 is where the Daopay compliance team picks it up.

  1. The application completes. All six application steps are done - business information, processing information, chargebacks and fraud, AML and CTF, contact information and documents.
  2. The agent generates both documents. The merchant contract and the Daopay KYC contract are generated together, by the PXP agent, from the application summary page.
  3. The agent sends the contract. The merchant contract goes out for signature.
  4. Screening checks run. Companies House, ComplyAdvantage, VMSS, MATCH, ID-Pal and Webshield complete, each result available as a PDF report.
  5. The contract is fully signed. Three signatures: the merchant, then the Daopay CEO and the Daopay CTO countersigning.
  6. The documents and CRM record upload to Daopay automatically. Full signature is the trigger; nobody presses anything. The equivalent manual controls stay on the page as a PXP fallback if a send needs repeating.
  7. Daopay compliance is notified. The application is now waiting on a Daopay decision.
  8. Daopay compliance opens the application. Their Applications list is the portal's own list, filtered to the applications where they are the acquirer.
  9. They make one of three decisions. Reject, pending further information, or approve - set out below.
  10. The merchant signs the KYC. One signature. That completes the onboarding.

The list of applications is scoped the same way. A Daopay user sees the portal's Applications table filtered to the applications where Daopay is the acquirer, not the full book.

Two statuses, and why they differ

There are two status fields on the page and they are not the same thing. An application can legitimately show one value at the top and a different one in the card.

  • The onboarding stage is the chip at the top of the page and in the list's STATUS column, derived from where the application has got to: Application In Progress, Awaiting Contract Send, Awaiting Contract Signature, Onboarding: Pending MID, Approved. Nobody sets it directly.
  • The decision status is the Application status select inside the card, set by a person: Draft, In Progress, Submitted, Pending Manual Review, Approved, Rejected, Pending Further Information, Cancelled. The first four are not selectable from this point in the flow.

A Daopay decision changes the second. The first catches up on its own.

Signatures

Two documents, two different ceremonies. Mixing them up is the easiest mistake to make when reading the contracts tables.

  • The merchant contract takes three signatures: the merchant, then the Daopay CEO and the Daopay CTO countersigning. It is not fully signed, and the automatic upload does not fire, until all three are in.
  • The Daopay KYC contract takes one: the merchant's. Nobody countersigns.

Getting help
