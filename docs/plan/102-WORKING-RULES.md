# 102 - Working rules for both sessions

The two rules that govern every wave in the presentation-readiness
programme, whichever session is running. They live here rather than in
docs/plan/100-PRESENTATION-READINESS.md because both sessions cite them
and a rule stated in the file of one of the two readers is half-homed.

The accuracy discipline says what may be written at all. The propagation
rule says how an answer, once written, spreads. Read together they are
the answer to "how do we know the content is relevant and right".

Public repo: process only.

## The accuracy discipline

This says what may be written at all; the propagation rule below says
how it spreads once written.

**1. Derive before asking; cite what you derived from.** Finding 9
measured that 124 of the 149 rows carry details, a note, a link or a
source document. Those are drafted from their own material, and the
draft records which - so a later reader can check the reasoning rather
than only the conclusion.

**2. The twenty-five with no evidence are asked, never drafted.** A row
with nothing but a title gives a drafter nothing to be right about.
Those are owner-answered or recorded as a `work_notes` question. This is
the rule most likely to be broken under time pressure, which is why it
is stated as a count rather than a principle: 25 rows, nameable, and the
list is checked at close.

**3. Evidence travels with the claim, not the other way round.** An
answer generalises to another row only where the same evidence holds -
not where the titles rhyme. Semantic similarity is how a benefit written
for one workstream ends up on four that merely share vocabulary, and the
embedding index makes that mistake easy and invisible. Propagation is by
parentage, by theme, by an explicit link, or by the owner saying so.

**4. A draft that does not clear the bar is rejected, not softened.**
The bar is in 110: a named cost, a named failure or a named obligation,
and a named beneficiary. Rewriting a weak draft into vaguer language to
make it defensible produces the worst artefact available - something
that survives review by saying nothing.

**5. Contradictions are raised, not resolved.** A benefit that implies a
department the row does not carry, an ownership call that fights the
theme's owner, an inherited benefit that does not fit the child: each is
a reconciliation finding for the owner, not a judgement call for the
session.

**6. Confirmed means the owner said so.** Not "the owner did not object
to a batch of eight". Silence on a wave leaves those rows `drafted` and
counted, and the close-out reports the number.

## The propagation rule

The owner's requirement that answers apply "throughout other related
work items" needs to be mechanical or it will not happen. The rule:

Every answer is recorded once at the highest level it is true, and
inherited downward. A benefit agreed for a workstream frames every child
before any child is asked about. An ownership rule agreed for one theme
applies to all its rows before the next theme is opened. A department
association agreed for a parent is proposed for every child.

Where an answer contradicts something already stored - a benefit that
implies a department the row does not carry, an ownership call that
contradicts a theme's owner - that is a **reconciliation finding**, and
it is raised, not silently resolved. Where an answer generalises beyond
its row, it is written as a `work_notes` decision anchored to the
workstream, and a `knowledge_links` row typed `relates_to`, `about` or
`affects` where it connects two records. A link written by an assistant
is `proposed` until confirmed in as many words.

Where the owner does not know, the answer is a `work_notes` row with
`kind='question'` anchored to the item. A recorded question is a better
state than a confident sentence nobody checked.
